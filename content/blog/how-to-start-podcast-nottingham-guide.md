# How to Start a Podcast in Nottingham: The Complete 2024 Guide

*Everything you need to know, from equipment anxiety to your first 1,000 downloads*

---

So you want to start a podcast in Nottingham. Congratulations. You've chosen a medium that's growing, engaging, and (contrary to popular belief) not actually that difficult to break into. You've also chosen a city that's perfectly positioned for podcasting success: big enough to have stories worth telling, small enough that you can actually get access to the people telling them.

This guide will take you from zero to published podcast. Not in the vague "follow your dreams" sense, but in the practical "do these specific things in this specific order" sense. By the end, you'll have a podcast that exists, sounds professional, and has a plausible path to finding an audience.

## Phase 1: The Foundation (Before You Spend Any Money)

### Step 1: Define Your Podcast (The Most Important Step)

Most podcast failures happen before recording starts. They happen because the podcaster never answered three fundamental questions:

**Who is this for?**
Not "everyone interested in [topic]." That's not an audience, that's a wish. Be specific: "Marketing managers at Nottingham SMEs who want practical growth strategies they can implement this week." Or: "People who moved to Nottingham in their 30s and want to feel connected to the city." Or: "Nottingham Forest fans who want tactical analysis, not just match reactions."

The more specific your audience, the easier everything else becomes. Content decisions, guest selection, marketing channels - they all flow from audience clarity.

**What transformation are you offering?**
Every successful podcast promises a transformation. The listener starts in one state (uninformed, unmotivated, disconnected) and ends in another (informed, inspired, connected). What is that transformation for your podcast?

Examples:
- "From confused about local politics to informed and engaged citizen"
- "From struggling with fitness to consistently active and motivated"
- "From isolated creative to connected member of Nottingham's art scene"

**Why you?**
There are 4 million podcasts in the world. Why should anyone listen to yours? What unique perspective, access, or expertise do you bring? This isn't about being the world's foremost expert. It's about having a specific angle that others don't.

Maybe you have access to people others don't (you're a journalist, you work in a specific industry, you're connected to a community). Maybe you have a specific perspective (you're an immigrant seeing the city with fresh eyes, you're a historian with deep context, you're a comedian finding the absurd in the ordinary). Maybe you have a specific format (short daily episodes, long-form interviews, narrative storytelling).

Whatever it is, know it. It's your competitive advantage.

### Step 2: Choose Your Format

There are four main podcast formats, each with different production requirements:

**Solo Shows:**
- You talking directly to the audience
- Pros: Complete control, easy to schedule, establishes your expertise
- Cons: Harder to grow (no guest audiences), can feel lonely, requires strong performance skills
- Best for: Experts sharing knowledge, personal storytelling, daily short-form content

**Interview Shows:**
- You interviewing guests
- Pros: Built-in audience expansion (guests share), easier to create (conversation flows naturally), establishes your network
- Cons: Scheduling complexity, quality depends on guest performance, requires good interviewing skills
- Best for: Building authority, network expansion, exploring diverse perspectives

**Co-hosted Shows:**
- You and a regular co-host
- Pros: Natural chemistry, shared workload, conversational energy
- Cons: Scheduling complexity, potential for conflict, requires compatible styles
- Best for: Conversational topics, comedy, debate formats

**Narrative/Documentary:**
- Storytelling with multiple voices, scenes, and production elements
- Pros: Highly engaging, stands out, awards potential
- Cons: Extremely time-intensive, requires high production skills, expensive
- Best for: Investigative journalism, historical storytelling, unique narratives

For your first podcast, I recommend either solo or interview format. They're the most sustainable and have the clearest paths to growth.

### Step 3: Plan Your First 10 Episodes

Before you record anything, plan your first 10 episodes. Not in vague terms - actual titles and descriptions. This serves three purposes:

1. **It proves you have enough to say.** If you can't come up with 10 episode ideas now, you won't have enough content to sustain a podcast.

2. **It reveals your format.** The planning process will show you whether your ideas work better as solo episodes, interviews, or something else.

3. **It gives you momentum.** When you start recording, you'll know exactly what's next. No staring at a blank page wondering what to talk about.

## Phase 2: The Technical Setup (Without Breaking the Bank)

### Option A: The Professional Studio Route (Recommended)

Here's an interesting fact: the cost difference between a basic home setup and professional studio hire is smaller than you think, especially when you factor in your time.

**Basic home setup:**
- Microphone: £100-200
- Headphones: £50-100
- Recording software: £0-50/month
- Acoustic treatment: £100-300
- Camera (for video): £400-800
- Lighting: £100-200
- **Total: £750-1,650 upfront + ongoing software costs**
- **Time per episode: 6-10 hours (recording + editing)**

Plus, the studio sounds better. Significantly better. And you don't have to learn audio engineering.

### Option B: The Minimal Home Setup

If you genuinely want to record at home, here's the absolute minimum viable setup:

**Essential Equipment:**
- **Microphone:** Audio-Technica ATR2100x-USB (£80) or Samson Q2U (£70)
  - These are dynamic microphones that reject room noise better than condensers
  - USB connection means no audio interface needed
  - Professional podcasters use these for travel/recording

- **Headphones:** Audio-Technica ATH-M20x (£45) or any closed-back headphones
  - Closed-back prevents sound leaking into the microphone
  - You need to hear yourself while recording

- **Recording Software:** Audacity (free) or GarageBand (free on Mac)
  - Don't overthink this. Basic recording is all you need.

- **Recording Environment:** Your closet
  - Seriously. Clothes absorb sound. It's the best free acoustic treatment.
  - Or record in your car (parked, obviously). Surprisingly good acoustics.

**Total cost: £125-200**

This setup will get you 80% of the way to professional sound. The remaining 20% comes from:
- Room treatment (expensive and complicated)
- Better microphones (expensive and unnecessary at this stage)
- Professional mixing (time-consuming to learn)

Which is why, again, I recommend the professional studio route. But if budget is genuinely tight, this minimal setup will work.

### Option C: The Hybrid Approach

Record interviews at the studio (for quality), record solo episodes at home (for convenience). This gives you professional quality for your most important content while maintaining flexibility for quick-turnaround episodes.

## Phase 3: Recording Your First Episode

### Before You Record

**Script or Outline?**
Solo episodes need either a full script or detailed outline. Interview episodes need questions and research on your guest. Don't wing it. Winging it sounds like winging it.

**Test Everything**
Record a 2-minute test. Listen back. Check:
- Audio levels (not too quiet, not clipping)
- Background noise (air conditioning, traffic, neighbours)
- Your voice (do you sound natural or nervous?)
- Technical issues (pops, clicks, dropouts)

**Prepare Your Guest (If Applicable)**
Send your guest:
- The questions you'll ask (or at least topics)
- Technical requirements (headphones, quiet space, good internet for remote)
- What to expect (length, format, editing process)

### During Recording

**Energy and Pace**
Your recorded voice will sound 20% less energetic than it feels. If you feel like you're being slightly too enthusiastic, you're probably at the right level. If you feel natural, you sound bored.

**Pace yourself.** Nervous speakers rush. Take breaths. Pause between thoughts. These can be edited out later if they're too long, but they can't be added if they're not there.

**Don't Stop for Mistakes**
If you stumble, pause, take a breath, and restart the sentence. Don't stop recording. The pause gives you an edit point. Stopping and restarting creates technical headaches.

**Record Room Tone**
At the end of your session, record 30 seconds of silence in your recording space. This gives you (or your editor) a noise sample for noise reduction.

### After Recording

**Backup Immediately**
Copy your files to at least two locations before doing anything else. Cloud storage, external drive, whatever. Just don't lose your recording.

**Listen Back**
You don't need to listen to everything, but check the first 5 minutes and last 5 minutes. Make sure the recording worked before you release your guest or leave the studio.

## Phase 4: Editing and Production

### If You're Editing Yourself

**Software Options:**
- **Free:** Audacity, GarageBand
- **Paid:** Adobe Audition (£20/month), Hindenburg (£100 one-time), Descript (£12/month)

**Basic Editing Workflow:**
1. **Import and organise** - Label your tracks, set up your project
2. **Rough cut** - Remove obvious mistakes, long pauses, false starts
3. **Fine cut** - Tighten transitions, remove filler words (um, ah, you know)
4. **Mix** - Balance levels, EQ voices, add compression
5. **Master** - Final loudness adjustment, export to podcast standard (-16 LUFS stereo, -19 LUFS mono)

**Time Expectation:**
Plan for 3-4 hours of editing per 1 hour of recording for your first few episodes. With practice, you'll get faster. Or you'll decide that your time is better spent on content strategy and book a studio that does live editing.

### If You're Using a Studio with Live Editing

Walk out with finished content. Review it, approve it, upload it. Total time: 30 minutes post-recording.

This is not an exaggeration. This is what live editing means. The studio handles steps 1-5 while you're recording. You just need to review and approve.

## Phase 5: Publishing and Distribution

### Choose a Hosting Platform

Your podcast needs a host - a service that stores your audio files and generates your RSS feed. Options:

**Buzzsprout** (£10-20/month)
- Pros: Easy to use, great analytics, good support
- Cons: Limited storage on lower tiers
- Best for: Beginners who want simplicity

**Transistor** (£15-75/month)
- Pros: Professional features, unlimited storage, great for multiple shows
- Cons: More expensive
- Best for: Serious podcasters, businesses

**Anchor** (Free)
- Pros: Free, owned by Spotify, easy to use
- Cons: Limited features, you don't own your RSS feed
- Best for: Hobbyists, testing the waters

**Captivate** (£15-75/month)
- Pros: Growth-focused features, marketing tools
- Cons: Newer platform, smaller community
- Best for: Podcasters focused on growth

I recommend Buzzsprout for most beginners. It's the right balance of features, ease of use, and price.

### Submit to Directories

Once your host is set up, submit your podcast to:

1. **Apple Podcasts** (still the biggest directory)
2. **Spotify** (growing fast, especially internationally)
3. **Google Podcasts** (integrated with Android)
4. **Amazon Music/Audible** (growing audience)

Your host will have guides for each submission. It's usually just a matter of copying your RSS feed URL and filling out a form.

### Create Show Notes

Every episode needs show notes. These serve multiple purposes:
- SEO (search engines index your show notes)
- Accessibility (deaf/hard-of-hearing audiences can read content)
- Resources (links to things mentioned in the episode)
- Engagement (calls to action, contact info)

**Show Notes Template:**
- Episode title and number
- Brief description (2-3 sentences)
- Key topics/timestamps
- Links to resources mentioned
- Guest bio and links (if applicable)
- Call to action (subscribe, review, contact)

## Phase 6: Marketing and Growth

### The Launch Strategy

Don't just publish and hope. Plan a launch:

**Pre-Launch (2-4 weeks before):**
- Create social media accounts for your podcast
- Post teaser content
- Build an email list of people who want to know when you launch
- Reach out to potential guests for your first month of episodes

**Launch Week:**
- Release 3 episodes on day one (gives new listeners something to binge)
- Post on all your personal social accounts
- Email everyone you know
- Ask friends to subscribe and review (reviews help with Apple Podcasts algorithm)

**Post-Launch:**
- Consistent release schedule (weekly is ideal, biweekly is acceptable)
- Social media promotion for every episode
- Guest promotion (make it easy for guests to share)
- Community engagement (respond to comments, emails, messages)

### Nottingham-Specific Marketing Opportunities

**Local Media:**
- Nottingham Post (local news coverage)
- BBC Radio Nottingham (pitch yourself as a guest/expert)
- Nottingham Trent University and University of Nottingham student media
- LeftLion (local culture magazine)

**Local Events:**
- Creative Nottingham meetups
- Business networking events
- University events (often open to the public)
- Local conferences and festivals

**Local Partnerships:**
- Other Nottingham podcasters (cross-promotion)
- Local businesses (sponsorship opportunities)
- Creative spaces (StreamToday Studios, Antenna, Metronome)
- Community organisations

### The Content Multiplication Strategy

One recording session should produce multiple pieces of content:

**From one 60-minute episode:**
- The full episode (obviously)
- 3-5 short audio clips (30-60 seconds) for social media
- 5-10 quote graphics
- A blog post (episode summary + transcript)
- An email newsletter
- Stories/reels for Instagram/TikTok

This is how you fill a content calendar without creating new content every day. It's also why video podcasts are valuable - more content formats from the same recording.

## Phase 7: Monetisation (The Long Game)

Don't think about monetisation for your first 10 episodes. Focus on creating great content and building an audience. But know what's possible:

**Sponsorships:**
- Typically need 1,000+ downloads per episode to attract sponsors
- Nottingham local businesses may sponsor earlier (local audience is valuable)
- Use our [Podcast Sponsorship Agreement Template](/resources/sponsorship-agreement)

**Affiliate Marketing:**
- Recommend products you actually use
- Amazon Associates, podcast hosting affiliates, equipment affiliates
- Works with smaller audiences than sponsorships

**Premium Content:**
- Bonus episodes, early access, ad-free versions
- Patreon, Apple Podcasts Subscriptions, Spotify Paid Podcasts
- Requires engaged audience, not just large audience

**Services:**
- Consulting, coaching, speaking
- Your podcast establishes authority; your services monetise it
- Often the most profitable path for business podcasts
